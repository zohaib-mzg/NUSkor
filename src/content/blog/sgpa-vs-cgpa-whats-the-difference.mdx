Result season produces two numbers and one recurring argument: SGPA versus CGPA. They look similar, they move together, and they are constantly confused for each other — sometimes by students, occasionally by software, and once or twice by a group chat poll. The difference is worth ten minutes, because the two numbers answer genuinely different questions.

## The definitions, plainly

**SGPA** — Semester Grade Point Average — describes exactly one semester. It is the credit-weighted average of the grade points you earned in that term: quality points for the semester divided by the semester's credit hours.

**CGPA** — Cumulative Grade Point Average — describes everything so far. It is all quality points across every counted course divided by all counted credit hours.

The relationship between them is simpler than it looks: CGPA is the running total, and each SGPA is one instalment. A semester whose average sits above your CGPA pulls it up; a semester below drags it down. There is no other mechanism.

## They do not average together

Here is the mistake that breaks every attempt to reason about the pair. Two SGPAs of 3.10 and 2.80 do not combine to 2.95 — unless the two semesters carried exactly the same credit load. The combination is always:

**CGPA = (semester 1 quality points + semester 2 quality points + …) ÷ (total credit hours)**

Suppose semester one ended at 3.10 over 15 credit hours (46.5 quality points) and semester two at 2.80 over 12 credit hours (33.6 quality points). The running CGPA is:

**(46.5 + 33.6) ÷ 27 = 80.1 ÷ 27 = 2.97**

Not 2.95. The difference of 0.02 looks small until it sits between you and a rounding-sensitive target — and on larger imbalances the error grows. The reason is structural: a plain average of SGPAs weights each semester equally, while reality weights each semester by its credit hours.

## Which one matters, and when

Both matter, but they answer different questions.

- **SGPA is a diagnostic.** It tells you how this specific term went — useful for spotting whether a study change actually worked. Two SGPAs trending up is evidence; one is noise.
- **CGPA is the record.** Applications, eligibility checks and graduation status tend to look at the cumulative figure, because it is the only one that describes the whole degree. A spectacular semester is a nice story; the CGPA is the number that travels.
- **Both are inputs to planning.** To know what a target requires, you need your CGPA, your completed credits, and the credits still ahead — that trio. The [CGPA improvement planner](/tools/cgpa-improvement-planner) turns those three inputs into a required average, and the [GPA calculator](/tools/gpa-calculator) computes each semester's contribution as results land.

One practical consequence: when your SGPA and CGPA disagree — a great term but a flat cumulative number — the CGPA is not being stubborn. It is being weighted. Early semesters feel light because few credits exist to move; by the end, each semester moves the cumulative figure less. That pendulum is why the *start* of a degree matters more to the final number than most students realise, and why the [CGPA calculator](/tools/cgpa-calculator) is worth running with past semesters folded in.

### A concrete scenario

Suppose you are three semesters in with SGPAs of 2.80, 3.05 and 3.30 — an improving story. The cumulative number, though, depends on the semester loads. If the terms carried 18, 15 and 12 credits, the CGPA is (50.4 + 45.75 + 39.6) ÷ 45 = 135.75 ÷ 45 = 3.02, barely above the earliest semester's level despite two strong terms. Had the same grades sat on reversed loads — 12, 15 and 18 — the combination would be (33.6 + 45.75 + 59.4) ÷ 45 = 138.75 ÷ 45 = 3.08. Same performance, different arithmetic history, a visible difference in the number that travels with you. This is why “my CGPA doesn't move” usually has a load-based explanation rather than a psychological one.

## A quick planning habit

At the end of each semester, do three things in five minutes: check your SGPA against the previous term, update your cumulative CGPA with the new credits, and note the gap between your CGPA and your target (if you have one). Three numbers, one screenshot, done. Next semester you will know whether the gap is closing before it is fashionable to panic.

## FAQ

### Is a high SGPA better than a high CGPA?

Neither is “better” — they describe different scopes. A high SGPA with a low CGPA means recent success against an old deficit; a high CGPA with a low SGPA means strong history that just took a hit. What matters is the gap between your current trajectory and your target.

### Does an internship semester affect my CGPA?

Only if your institution awards credit hours and grades for it. Grading conventions for internships vary widely — some are pass/fail, some award grade points, some award credit without points. Confirm the convention before budgeting its effect.

### Why does my CGPA barely move anymore?

Because it is a weighted average of everything you have completed. With many completed credits, a single semester — unless extremely good or bad — shifts the cumulative number by a fraction. The further you are, the smaller each semester's pull; the [improvement planner](/tools/cgpa-improvement-planner) quantifies exactly how large your remaining lever is.

## The takeaway

SGPA is one semester's verdict; CGPA is the running record. Combine them the only correct way — total quality points over total credit hours — and never average the SGPAs directly. Understand that, and the two numbers stop arguing with each other and start telling you what to do next.
